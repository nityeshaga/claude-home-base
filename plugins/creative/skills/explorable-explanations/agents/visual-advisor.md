# Brief: visual advisor

**Inputs:** `persona.md`, `storyboard.md` with the x-factor entries filled in
(`references/specs.md` §4), and `references/design-patterns.md`. Nothing else — no HTML has
been built yet, and you don't need the design plan.
**Output:** `review/visual-advice.md`.

Read the tree as a whole first, then go card by card and look for the following. Be
concrete: name the page, say what you'd do instead, and say what it would let the reader see
or understand that the current choice doesn't.

- **A stronger version of the same idea.** The medium is right but the figure could be
  sharper — a better cut of the data, a more telling frame, the comparison drawn as one
  picture instead of two, the label that turns a diagram into an argument. Sometimes the
  step up is small motion rather than a redraw: a hover that names a part, a pointer that
  reveals the value under it, a detail that resolves as the reader moves through the figure.
- **A better medium.** A page whose idea would land harder as something else: the process
  that wants numbered frames rather than prose, the claim that wants real data, the thing
  the reader keeps trying to picture that wants a photo or a screenshot.
- **A playable worth building.** A page where operating the system is what would make the
  idea land — a surprising result the reader should produce themselves, a space worth poking
  around in, a decision they should feel. Say what the reader would do, what would surprise
  them, and roughly what rules it would need (`design-patterns.md` §2).
- **Something to cut.** A visual that decorates rather than explains, two figures doing one
  job, a playable proposed where a picture would convince.
- **The tree as a whole.** Does one kind of figure dominate to the point of monotony? Is
  there a stretch of pages where nothing is worth looking at? Does the sequence of visuals
  build — do later figures reuse the reader's familiarity with earlier ones?

Order your suggestions with the ones you feel strongest about first.
